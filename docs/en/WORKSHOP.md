# Character workshop

How to add your own plant to Sprout Squad. The reference is **Potling** (`sproutsquad:potling`), the mod's mascot: a simple "sun producer" on which the whole path from registration to texture was tested.

Below: how to come up with a character, the technical steps, the checklist and the art style guide.

## Coming up with a character

1. **Drawing.** A sketch on paper; the photo goes to `art/<id>/sketch.png`.
2. **Card.** Filled in from the template in `docs/en/characters/README.md`. The role is picked from mechanics the mod already has: shoots, lobs, explodes, takes hits, makes sun, sleeps and wakes others, fires a beam, summons helpers, digs, flies, walks through walls, carries a shield.
3. **Palette.** Take the 3–6 main colours of the drawing and fit them to the mod palette (see the style guide below).
4. **Model.** The model is built in Blockbench from the drawing and the texture is painted following the guide; one "funny detail" of the drawing is kept.
5. **Voice.** Sounds: planting, attack, sleep, defeat (a voice recorded on a phone works).
6. **Code.** The class following the steps below, registration, loot, en/ru, spawning or a recipe.
7. **Playtest.** A test game; the numbers in JSON and the config (`sunCosts`) are tuned until the character behaves as intended.
8. **Done.** A screenshot, status `done` on the card.

For a first go, a variant is easier — a new texture for an existing mob: done in one evening.

## Steps

1. **Class.** Copy `Potling.kt`, rename it, set up the AI goals. Ready-made goals from the original: `ProjectileAttackGoal` (shooter, like Peashooter), `GenerateSunGoal` (sun, like Sunflower), plus explosion, melee and so on in `joshxviii.plantz.ai.goal`. Sleeping at night — `sleepsDuringNight()`.
2. **Registration.** A single line `plant("<id>", ::<Name>, width, height, sunCost, attributes)` in `SquadEntities`. The seed packet in the creative tab, the sun cost and the spawn rules (on the ground, like the original's plants; another rule goes in `spawnRules`) appear automatically.
3. **Model.** A subclass of `SquadPlantModel`: geometry in `createBodyLayer()`, animations are passed to the constructor (`init`, `idle`, `action`, `sleep` — the required minimum from the guide). A Blockbench model (Modded Entity format, Java export, Mojang mappings) translates to Kotlin one-to-one: the same `addBox`, `PartPose`, `Keyframe`.
4. **Renderer.** Two lines in `SproutSquadClient`: the model layer and `PlantRenderer(...)`.
5. **Textures.** Main and `_sleep` (closed eyes) — 64×64 following the style guide below; the 16×16 icon matches the size of the original's icons. The original drawing goes in `art/<id>/`.
6. **Data.** Name in en/ru, a recipe with the `minecraft:entity_data` component, a line in `squad_plants.json`.
7. **Check.** `./gradlew runGameTestServer` — the `sproutsquad:squad_plants` test goes through the checklist and names what is missing: class, health, cost, spawn rules, tag, recipe, textures, icon, names. The `/pz gallery` gallery picks up the new character by itself.
8. **In game.** `./gradlew runClient`: plant it, watch the animations by day and at night, compare it with its neighbors in the gallery.
9. **Card.** `docs/en/characters/<id>.md` (and the Russian one): status, stats, pictures and the index: run `python3 tools/docs/refresh.py` (needs Pillow and numpy). It renders whatever the docs ask for that has no picture yet (the character from its model and texture, its looks named in a looks table as `Name (`look`)`, items, effects and structures named in tables or listed in a `<!-- pictures: … -->` line), puts the pictures into the cards and FEATURES, rebuilds `characters/README.md` and lists what of the mod still has no picture. `--force` renders everything again, `--check` only reports missing pictures.

## Character checklist

1. The card `docs/en/characters/<id>.md` is filled in and agreed with the author of the idea.
2. Model and texture (Blockbench, Java model export with Mojang mappings).
3. Entity class: a subclass of `Plant` (or of the zombie base class), AI goals, attributes.
4. Registration: `EntityType`, attributes, seed packet or spawn egg, renderer, model layer.
5. Sounds (optional) and `sounds.json`.
6. Translations: `en_us` and `ru_ru` — **required**.
7. How to get it in game: a recipe, loot, trading or seed mutation (`seed_mutation`); an entry in the creative tab.
8. For a zombie — spawning: a biome modifier and/or raids.
9. Check: single player and a dedicated server, no errors in the log.
10. A screenshot in `docs/characters/<id>.png`, status `done` on the card.

**Balance.** Health, damage and cost within ±30% of the closest counterpart in the mod, unless the author of the idea wants otherwise.

## Style guide

**Grid and size**
- 1 texel = 1/16 of a block; no "HD" details inside cubes.
- Canvas: **64×64 by default**; 32×32 for small characters (under 0.6 blocks); 128×128 only for big ones (Gargantuar class and up). Fit the canvas to the UV layout, not "with room to spare".
- A plant is 0.5–1.2 blocks, up to 30 cubes. A zombie is about 2 blocks. A boss is up to 3.5 blocks.

**Palette** (`art/palette.png`, generated by `art/draw_palette.py`)
- **Plants:** 3–4 tones per material (shadow, base, light, highlight), saturated; 12–30 colours in total. Base greens are the Peashooter scale: `#2d5213`, `#3f6d1f`, `#4a7e26`, `#79b930`, `#84c738`, `#98e244`, `#a7ec58`, `#bdf77c`. Sun: `#f6cf37`, `#ecb427`, `#f7e64d`, orange shadows `#cb6215`, `#d96c1c`. Mushroom stems: `#9cb989`, `#afca9d`, `#c7e0b6`.
- **Zombies:** skin, jacket and trousers from the Browncoat base colours (skin `#2f5434`–`#567157`, jacket `#2f2110`–`#47351e`, trousers `#2e2f4e`–`#3a3c63`), 4–5 tones per material, vanilla-style noise is fine. Every zombie has **one bright signature accent** (newspaper, afro, lamp, wheelbarrow) that makes it recognisable from afar.
- Draw only with the palette colours plus at most three of your own per character.

**Light and volume**
- Light from above: top faces lighter, bottom faces 1–2 tones darker; a 1-pixel highlight on the "cheek" and in the eye.
- No black outline along cube edges (vanilla style). Instead the outermost row of pixels is one tone darker.
- Noise: plants have at most two tones per patch, zombies use vanilla-style noise.

**Faces**
- Plants: 2×2 or 2×3 dark eyes (`#111f0a`) with a 1-pixel white highlight, a 1–2 pixel mouth. The `_sleep` texture has closed eyes drawn as dashes.
- Zombies: light eye whites with a dark pupil, a crooked mouth. Night zombies have emissive eyes.

**Emissive and glow**
- Only eyes, fire, electricity, magic; a separate `_emissive` file, at most 10% of the texture area.

**Required minimum**
- Textures: a plant — main and `_sleep`; a defender — `damage_low` and `damage_medium`; a zombie — main and `_baby` if a baby spawns.
- Animations: a plant — `idle`, `init` (appearing), `action`, `sleep`; a zombie — `idle`, `walk`, `attack`; a boss — plus a special attack or summon and an entrance.
- Sounds: planting or appearing, attack, hurt, defeat.

**Readability check**
- Look at it in game from 16 blocks by day and by night: the silhouette differs from its neighbours, the signature accent is visible.
- Put it next to a reference (Citron, Sunflower, Super Brainz) — it does not stand out.

**Turning drawings into art**
- Take the 3–6 main colours of the drawing, simplify the shapes into blocks, and always keep one "funny detail" from the drawing: that keeps the character recognisable to its author.

## Why it is done this way

- Our own code registers through `DeferredRegister` rather than a direct `Registry.register` like the original: this way there is no need to wait for the last `RegisterEvent`, and our own objects can be touched freely.
- `SquadPlantModel` only bakes the animations into the original's `PlantModel` fields (they were made `protected` for this), while the original itself plays them. If the author changes the plant animation logic, our plants will get it along with his plants.
- The sun cost is set in `SquadEntities` and on first access is written to the server config (`config/sproutsquad/server.json`), as with the original's plants. From then on the config value applies: it can be tweaked without rebuilding, and a new cost in code does not affect an already created config — fix the line in `server.json` or delete it.
